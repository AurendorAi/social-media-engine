## Evidence boundaries

A qualifying record required every advertised page and alternative to be visually inspected at readable resolution. Thumbnails, search cards, repeated IDs and partial previews do not count. Static source formats included posts, decks, covers, documents and other formats; their social adaptations are proposals, not tested outcomes. Exact fonts, production processes and audience reactions were not generally verified. These counts describe the recorded study, not a new inspection of source pixels during release preparation.

Native Canva filters were context-dependent. A query term is not a native filter, a title is not visual evidence, and visual resemblance is not historical authentication. For example, the observed Japandi and Brutalism records are qualified contemporary hybrids; Minimalism has zero credited records even though Minimalist has four. Do not silently merge near-synonyms. Candidate exclusions are useful counterexamples, not extra style knowledge.

The [sanitized evidence index](../assets/research-evidence.json) contains counts and a curated attribution for each observed category. It contains no preview images, downloaded materials, private paths, source-ledger dumps or customer files. Source titles identify works; no template marketing copy is licensed or recommended for reuse. Reopen an accessible source before making a new source-specific claim. Check rights independently before importing any material.

## Quality and fit are separate

A well-matched style can contain filler, false claims, unreadable type or irrelevant imagery. An excellent execution can still be wrong for the owner’s identity or audience. First assess evidence and communication; then style fit; then craft at the delivered size. Never let an average score excuse a false claim or a missing central payoff. Do not infer results from attractive previews.

No motion principle is validated by this study: there were zero complete audiovisual reviews. Follow the actual video production and independent review requirements rather than extrapolating animation timing from static frames.
