# An editorial method that produces useful, recognizable work

## Find the thought before the hook

For each idea, write a compact research note in the private workspace:
- Real audience situation: an observed question, friction, decision or desire.
- The distinction or observation the post makes, and why the audience should care now.
- Evidence: verified source, date, relevant passage/observation, limits and claim supported.
- Worked example, demonstration, checklist or decision the reader can use afterward.
- The brand's point of view and why this brand is a credible source for this subject.

An owner's story is not a measured outcome; a public question is not a customer case;
a fictional example is not a recorded experiment. Label each honestly. Recheck changing
facts near production. If a claim fails, replace the argument or research again before
polishing its wording. Preserve source notes rather than a giant unsorted bookmark dump.

Weak: “We help your business be more efficient.”
Useful starting angle: “Your team enters the same order twice. Which record should be
trusted when the quantity changes?” Then show the actual distinction and consequence.
This is a method example, not a universal B2B subject. A bakery might explain an ordering
choice; a ceramicist might reveal a material decision; a teacher might clarify a common
misconception. Use the real brand, audience and level of knowledge.

## Build an argument, not a pile of tips

A working structure is situation → meaningful distinction → evidence/example → useful
payoff → relevant next step. Change that structure when the subject needs a different
form. The payoff must fulfill the hook. Don't delay the central point for a cinematic
intro. A strong argument can be one image, a comparison, a walkthrough or a story.

Choose tone according to the situation: empathy when someone is anxious, restraint when
precision matters, humor when the observation earns it. A dialect marker is not a
personality. Don't invent lived experience to sound human or repeatedly use rhetorical
questions, dramatic fragments, oppositions, slogans and false urgency.

For long educational carousels, keep the substantive development. Cut repetition and
jargon, not the explanation that makes the post useful. Mix audience knowledge levels
when appropriate; don't turn a specialist brand into an endless beginner tips account.
A commercial CTA follows a complete payoff and an actual relevant offer.

## A month should have range

Use a planning matrix across audience need, knowledge level, objective, subject,
evidence type, format, emotional tone and visual mechanism. Look for repeated openings,
identical examples, overused layouts and four videos that all have the same duration,
scene rhythm and soundtrack. Shared fonts and colors create continuity; identical
storytelling does not. Do not impose a fixed educational/promotional ratio on every brand.

For each plan item, put the useful payoff, evidence to obtain, visual demonstration,
production dependency and expected audience response into the brief, alongside required
calendar fields. Use the optional `editorial` object to preserve this in plan JSON.
An experiment states a hypothesis and a real measure; it does not promise reach or sales.

## Edit against observable failures

Read the actual draft and ask:
1. Could an unrelated brand publish this after changing only the logo?
2. What specific new understanding, feeling or action will the reader leave with?
3. Does the example demonstrate the point, or merely mention the topic?
4. Can every factual claim be traced to evidence with the same scope?
5. Does the voice suit the people and situation, including the chosen language variety?
6. Does each slide/scene add something, and is the decisive detail large enough to read?

If the underlying thought is weak, choose rewrite/research/replace. Don't merely
paraphrase weak copy or swap a background. Keep/polish/redesign are appropriate when
the idea works and the problem is execution. Record the concrete failure and repair.

## Apply the same rigor to each brand

Follow [brand quality](brand-quality.md): establish the real baseline, save the selected
brand's creative brief, and connect audience situation, evidence, mechanism, payoff,
voice and meaningful materials. Transfer the method, never another brand's identity.
Check cross-brand layout repetition and repetitive CTA-only ideas before scaling.
Use [research findings](research-findings.md) as conditional observations, not templates
or sector-to-style assignments. Owner constraints and actual feedback take precedence.
